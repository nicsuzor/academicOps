A report handed back is second-hand the moment it arrives. Its evidence either
came attached or it did not — a result cannot be amended after it returns, and
nobody downstream can reconstruct what was never sent.

Every load-bearing claim carries one of two things:

1. **Checkable evidence** — the command run with its observed output, a
   `file:line`, a resolving URL, a quoted source, a commit hash — enough that the
   claim can be validated without reading the originating transcript.
2. **A stated failure reason.** Honest failure is a complete handback, not a
   defect: could not do X, because Y.

There is no third option. Label each claim **Observed** where you saw the primary
evidence yourself this session, **Reported** where you are passing on a subagent's,
a tool's, or a document's word — and name that source. Evidence that arrived
attributed and intact is not second-hand; forward it verbatim. A paraphrase of
someone else's proof is not proof.

**Attaching the proof is the worker's job**, done in the report itself as the
report is written.

**Judging a report that lacks it is the receiver's, and the only move is to send
it back**, naming the claim left unsupported. Do not verify it yourself, re-run
the work, or fill the gap: that spends your context on what you already paid for
and leaves the worker's standard exactly where it was.
