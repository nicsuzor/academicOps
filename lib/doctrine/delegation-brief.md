A brief buys judgment. Compose it around three elements, every one mandatory:
**goal**, **constraints**, **evidence bar**. A brief missing one of them is not
thin, it is broken — do not dispatch it.

### Goal — what to achieve, and why it matters

State the end, not the route. Say what will be true when the work has landed and
what it is for, so a worker who finds a better way to the same end can recognise
it as the same end. A numbered task list is not a goal: it forecloses the judgment
the delegation exists to buy, and it turns every unforeseen obstacle into a stall
or a lie.

Two things ride with the goal:

- **Scoped context** — what the worker needs to find its way and no more: where
  the relevant code, records, and prior decisions are, and which of them are
  already settled. What you leave out it rediscovers at your cost or invents.
  What you pile in it reads instead of thinking.
- **Effort type** — mechanical, exploratory, or judgment-heavy. This is what tells
  a worker whether to execute or to decide, and it picks the surface.

### Constraints — what holds whatever route is taken

Constraints are what make autonomy safe to grant: name them and the worker can be
let run, leave them implicit and you must supervise every step instead. State

- the law that binds this work — the axioms plus any local rule sharper than them;
- what must not be touched: files, branches, systems, another worker's lane;
- **autonomy and non-goals** — which decisions the worker makes on its own, which
  come back to you, and which adjacent good ideas are out of scope. A worker with
  no stated non-goals expands until something breaks.

Say what to do when a constraint and the goal collide: stop and report. A brief
that leaves that unstated gets it resolved silently in favour of finishing.

### Evidence bar — what makes a claim admissible

The handback doctrine fixes the shape every report must carry; the brief fixes the
bar for this unit. It is not the worker's to choose, and "I verified it" is not
evidence.

- **Acceptance criteria** — what must be true for the unit to be accepted, each
  written so that it names the evidence that would settle it. A criterion nothing
  can be shown against is not a criterion.
- Require the evidence on the task record, not only in the reply. The record is
  the only surface anything downstream reads.

A report asserting success without evidence is a failed brief, not a finished
unit. Raise the bar that let it through.

### Sizing

Every line either buys judgment or spends it. Cut whatever the worker can read for
itself from the context you pointed it at.
