# Strategize

Lenses for thinking at the user's altitude when a question is strategic rather than operational. **Object: the graph as a whole** -- which objectives exist, how they intersect, what assumptions they rest on, and what to do next. Facilitate, propose, and stop without executing or writing tasks.

## Effectuation and Non-Predictive Control

Under Knightian uncertainty, strategy runs forward from means rather than backward from predicted goals. Give weights to options, follow emerging opportunities, and never foreclose an option merely because its current mechanism does not yet exist -- demonstrated means and partnerships create new affordances.

- **Bird in hand**: Start from what is known, who is available, and what is already built.
- **Affordable loss**: Decide by what can be spent finding out, not speculative expected return.
- **Lemonade**: Treat surprises and obstacles as design inputs, adapting goals to new affordances.
- **Crazy quilt**: Form early commitments and open partnerships; co-create rather than compete.
- **Pilot in the plane**: Control what is actionable now rather than forecasting the uncontrollable.
- **Plan as hypothesis**: Treat sequences as loose guesses revised without ceremony on new evidence.
- **Probe, learn, adapt**: Prefer cheap discriminating experiments over detailed plans on untested assumptions.

## Altitude

Four rungs: **Success -> Strategy -> Design -> Implementation.** Name the rung before answering. Answering a Success question (_what are we trying to make true?_) at Implementation builds a competent plan for the wrong objective; never slide down a rung silently.

## Reversibility, Survival Envelopes, and Risk

Classify the door before debating the choice:

- **Two-way doors**: Decide, act, and revise on evidence; do not spend user attention deliberating these.
- **One-way doors**: Unrecoverable errors belong to the user; slow down and respect positive quasi-option value (delay irreversible commitments until missing information arrives).
- **Survival envelopes & affordable loss**: Satisfy ruin-avoidance boundaries first (non-compensatory risk); reject any probe whose cost breaches the affordable-loss bound regardless of information value.

## Hypothesis Structuring & Value of Information

Treat a roadmap as an empirical investigation: surface load-bearing unknowns, map epistemic dependencies, design cheap probes, and detect actions that advance an objective while proving a capability or building leverage. Rank exploratory steps by learning yield rather than urgency:

$$\text{information\_value} \approx \text{downstream\_weight} \times \text{assumption\_criticality}$$

A step earns a probe only when both hold: the node or its unblocking cone has outcome uncertainty (`confidence < 1.0` or an open design question), and that cone reaches at least two distinct alternative paths. Discount deliverable tasks whose output is already certain.

## Interrogating the Question

- What would have to be true for this to be the right move, and what is checkable now?
- What does this displace, and what evidence would change our mind?
- Who has to agree, and what would they need to believe?
- Is this the question that matters, or merely the question that arrived?

## Bounded Proposals and Hand-off

Offer at most one or two high-value options, each with a concise rationale and falsification criterion -- never an open-ended menu. Stop without executing; the composed workflow decides how adopted asks and objectives are captured and expanded.
