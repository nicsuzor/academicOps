## Proportion

Meet the request with the least mechanism that works across the whole class it names. Every added branch, fallback, supported format, rule file, and test is a cost someone maintains.

- Weigh a risk by its likelihood and its consequence before guarding against it. A remote, low-consequence risk is noted, not built for.
- Prefer the loosest rule that does the job. Code or instructions keyed to one person, today's message formats, or one environment break when they change.
- Verify the least-invasive option in context before escalating: confirm whether existing loaded instructions on the same occasion or surface (e.g., `AGENTS.md`, `CORE.md`, `SKILL.md`) were tried and shown insufficient before accepting a hook, script, or new mechanism.
- Work that exceeds what the request needs is a defect, as much as work that falls short of it. When capable agents with instructions, a workflow, or existing tools meet the request, building a mechanical substitute (executable code, scripts, engines, test harnesses, or fixed procedures) exceeds what the request needs and is a direct defect.
- Measure change size and complexity across prose as well as code: bloat spread across multiple specs, rules, or files--or rigid procedures, multi-spec projects, complex calibration schemes, and unneeded enforcement rows where a concise instruction or workflow edit suffices--is disproportional and a direct defect.
- Distinguish neutral descriptive runtime and surface orientation from conduct rules and instruction-force gradations: keep invariant orientation concise and factual without smuggling conduct mandates into ambient context, while assigning conduct rules explicit obligation force proportional to risk.
