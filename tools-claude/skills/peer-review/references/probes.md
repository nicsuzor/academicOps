# Analytical Probes

Read the application against these eleven core probes during PREP. Substitute disciplinary equivalents as appropriate.

## The Eleven Probes

1. **Theory of change / the missing bridge**: How empirical activities produce promised normative, policy, or design outcomes. Descriptive predictors to normative guidance must be designed, not merely asserted.
2. **Regulatory target clarity**: Exact definition of what is being regulated, who is the addressee, and whether jurisdictional comparisons serve the research question.
3. **Phenomenon vs research project**: Distinguish studying something new from defining a conceptual research problem.
4. **Conceptual contribution vs descriptive output**: Identify whether outputs provide theoretical advancement or merely descriptive summaries.
5. **Harm/problem assumed rather than demonstrated**: Stated urgency or risk must be grounded in empirical literature, not asserted.
6. **Construct validity & method-question fit**: Methods must legitimately measure claimed constructs (e.g. sample size vs generalization claims).
7. **Scope vs resourcing**: Balance ambition across jurisdictions and domains against allocated funded effort.
8. **Citation integration**: Literature deeply integrated into scaffolding rather than grouped into superficial citation bursts.
9. **Access and dependency risk**: Industry, community, or regulatory access grounded in demonstrable prior groundwork or fallback plans.
10. **Real translation pathways**: Measurable translation pathways (advisory boards, regulatory engagement, legislative impact) over generic public-facing resources.
11. **Institutional commitment quality**: Tangible resource allocation (cash vs in-kind, teaching load adjustments) and transition management.

## Operational Use in PREP

- Consider every probe; record firing probes as line-cited entries in reading notes ([[reading-notes-format]]).
- Identify the primary analytical vulnerability (typically Probes 1, 3, 4, or 6) to anchor overall evaluation.
- Differentiate feasibility/resourcing (Probes 7, 11) from access risk (Probe 9). Direct resourcing critiques to institutions, not early-career researchers.
