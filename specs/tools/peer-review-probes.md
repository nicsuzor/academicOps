# Peer-review analytical probes — why eleven, verbatim

`plugins/tools/skills/peer-review/references/probes.md` carries eleven analytical
probes, each held exactly as written rather than condensed into a shorter list.

## Why condensing loses probes, not just words

An eight-probe paraphrase of the same source set was tried and checked against the
source. It collapses two probes that are both feasibility-shaped — scope-vs-resourcing
and access-and-dependency-risk — into a single "feasibility & access" probe, and drops
four entirely:

- Conceptual contribution vs descriptive output
- Citation integration
- Real translation pathways
- Institutional commitment quality

Each dropped probe names a distinct analytical move the source reviewer makes and
applies independently of the others; a paraphrase short enough to read as a summary
cannot state all eleven without either merging near-neighbours or cutting the ones
that seem, at a glance, least load-bearing. Both moves are the same failure: the
reviewing agent loses a probe from its working repertoire, and nothing in the
paraphrase signals that a probe went missing.

## What this means for editors

Extend the set with probes a field needs; never prune or merge the existing eleven,
including under a general "tighten this file" instruction. A shorter restatement of
this set is not a lighter-weight version of the same content — it is a different,
smaller set that happens to share most of its wording with the original.
