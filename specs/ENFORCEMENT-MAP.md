# Enforcement Map

The register of what the framework currently makes agents do. Design intent —
why enforcement is shaped this way at all — is
[`enforcement.md`](enforcement/enforcement.md); this file is the current truth
and holds no argument.

**The permission surface and the rule roster are both dark.** Every axiom and
this repository's project rules carry `trigger: off`, and the `tools`, `skills`,
`subagents`, and skill-`agent:` grants have been removed. Enforcement is being
restored one rule at a time against observed failure, so the two restriction
columns below stay empty until something is put back into them. One entry
survives — `q`'s `allowed-tools` — and it is marked.

## Vocabulary

**Kind** — `agent` · `skill` · `hook` · `structural` (holds without the agent's
cooperation) · `rule` · `graph` (an obligation carried as a task node) ·
`ci` · `observability`.

**Tone** — how the instruction is written, which is the only lever most of these
mechanisms have:

- **absolute** — states no exception: "never", "only", "non-negotiable".
- **imperative** — a plain instruction to do the thing.
- **conditional** — do X when Y; every halt-and-report shape lands here.
- **cautionary** — names a hazard and leaves the call to the agent.
- **advisory** — offered to be weighed.

**Binding** — `binding` (an obligation a reviewer can cite the agent against) ·
`advisory` (injected to be weighed, overridable) · `structural` (does not depend
on compliance) · `inert` (declared, but nothing carries it).

**State** — `live` · `dark` (wired, zero content) · `disconnected` (registered
nowhere) · `unbuilt` · `external`.

## Not enforcement

Named here so the absence is deliberate rather than an omission.

- **`ts` Tailscale bring-up** (`SessionStart`) — connectivity for remote sessions.
- **`tools` capability skills** — `dbt`, `streamlit`, `python-viz`, `pdf`,
  `diagram`, `style`, `extract`, `new-project`, `deep-research`. Capabilities, not
  mechanisms: the restrictions on when they are used, and the instructions that
  point at them, are the enforcement.

## Known drift

- **`specs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, Hooks** describes the cope, rbg roster, rbg
  stop-gate, pkb grounding, and hearsay hooks as wired. All of them are
  commented out of their plugins' `HANDLERS`. That table is design intent; the
  register above is what fires.
- **No stop-time hook is declared `async` any more.** The one surviving `async`
  declaration is `orchestrate`'s `PostToolBatch`, which is itself commented out
  of `HANDLERS`. A disposition returned on a stop is therefore honoured, which
  is what makes `orchestrate`'s `block()` on `Stop` binding rather than
  advisory.
- **A disconnected hook still spawns a process.** The manifests continue to
  declare the events, so the client runs the dispatcher and it does nothing —
  dark and degraded are not distinguishable from outside.
